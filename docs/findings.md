# Safari Connect — Executive Findings

## Route Performance
- **RT001 (Nairobi–Mombasa)** is the flagship route: highest revenue (**KES 51,600**) and best seat efficiency (**KES 1,259 per seat sold**).
- **RT009 (Nairobi–Machakos)** is the weakest route: lowest revenue (**KES 7,300**) and fewest bookings (**20**).
- **RT005 (Nairobi–Thika)** attracts the most passengers (**30 bookings**) but has the lowest revenue efficiency (KES 123/seat) — low fares or underutilized revenue potential.

## Revenue & Demand Trends
- **Monday** is the strongest revenue day (**KES 47,350**); **Wednesday** has the highest demand (**52 bookings**).
- **Sunday** is the weakest day: **11 bookings**, **KES 8,835** revenue.
- Peak revenue comes from **19:00 departures (KES 22,240)**; **13:00 departures generate the least (KES 12,045)**.
- Best revenue months: **Oct 2024 (KES 23,680)**, **Jul 2024 (KES 22,855)**, **Nov 2024 (KES 20,270)**. Weakest: **Jan 2025 (KES 1,685)**, **Aug 2024 (KES 13,400)**, **Feb 2024 (KES 15,640)**.

## Fleet Performance
- **Matatus** are the most profitable fleet: **KES 87,925** from **97 bookings**.
- **Buses** follow with **KES 84,555** from **95 bookings**.
- **Minibuses** contribute the least revenue (**KES 55,330**) but deliver strong customer satisfaction.

## Driver Insights
- **Isaac Korir** is the top revenue driver: **KES 33,045** across **33 trips** — strongest promotion candidate.
- **Hassan Abdi** achieves the highest passenger satisfaction (**3.9/5**) and is the most balanced performer.
- **Moses Kipchoge** holds the highest driver rating (**4.8/5**) yet among the lowest passenger trip ratings (**3.2**) — driver rating does **not** strongly correlate with passenger satisfaction or revenue. Trip experience (punctuality, comfort, route length) matters more than conduct alone.

## Customer Insights
- **Economy Class dominates**: **79.4%** of bookings vs **20.6%** Business.
- **Female passengers generate more revenue (KES 120,885)** than male (KES 106,925).
- **Nairobi** is the strongest market: **113 bookings**, **KES 112,000** revenue.
- Satisfaction split: **46.2% satisfied**, **28.9% neutral**, **19.4% unsatisfied** — just below the 50% benchmark.

## Cancellations & Revenue Leakage
- **Mombasa–Malindi** has the highest cancellation rate (**18.5%**); **Nairobi–Eldoret** the lowest (**3.6%**).
- Total revenue lost: **KES 32,150** (Cancelled: KES 24,805 · No-shows: KES 7,345).
- **RT001 (Nairobi–Mombasa)** loses the most absolute revenue (**KES 10,800**) due to higher ticket prices, despite a lower cancellation rate.

## Capacity & Scheduling
- Busiest booking window: **06:00–09:00** — 09:00 (25 bookings), 06:00 (24 bookings). Demand slows 16:00–17:30.
- All vehicle types run at a **low average load factor (~2 seats per trip)** — excess capacity across the fleet.

---

## Recommendations
1. **Optimize fleet allocation** — deploy more vehicles in the 06:00–09:00 window; reduce frequency off-peak to lift load factors.
2. **Focus on high-value routes** — add capacity and premium offerings on RT001; review pricing/demand stimulation for RT009.
3. **Improve customer satisfaction** — target punctuality, vehicle comfort, and trip experience to push satisfaction above 50%.
4. **Reduce revenue leakage** — no refund for same-day cancellations/no-shows; 50% refund for cancellations ≥2 days before departure.
5. **Improve fleet utilization** — consolidate underperforming schedules; average occupancy of ~2 seats/trip leaves major revenue on the table.
6. **Leverage high-demand segments** — prioritize Economy Class, female travellers, and the Nairobi market.

**Executive takeaway:** the business is anchored by RT001 (Nairobi–Mombasa), Matatu operations, Economy-class travellers, and Nairobi-origin demand. The biggest growth levers are vehicle utilization, cancellation reduction, customer satisfaction, and aligning fleet capacity with peak demand (06:00–09:00 and 19:00 departures).
